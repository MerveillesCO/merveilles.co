# Merveilles Primitive Campground Project Plan

**Property:** 21085 Capella Drive, Monument, Colorado 80132  
**Parcel:** 7103005001  
**Owner and applicant:** Daniel McShan  
**Zoning:** RR-5  
**Project status:** Early feasibility and agency review  
**Last updated:** September 8, 2026

## 1. Project purpose

Merveilles is proposed as a small, reservation-only, fee-based primitive tent
campground on the owner's five-acre residential property. The first phase is
intended to establish the smallest viable operation, learn from actual use, and
minimize effects on neighbors, traffic, fire protection, utilities, and the
land.

The initial proposal does not include RVs, RV hookups, cabins, permanent lodging
structures, amplified events, or generators. Aspirations for yurts, bell tents,
or other furnished lodging are intentionally deferred. Any future lodging or
expansion would be evaluated as a separate phase and would proceed only after
the required land-use, building, health, fire, and wastewater approvals.

## 3. Proposed operating model

- Advance reservations only; no public walk-in camping
- Tent camping only during the initial phase
- Defined campsite boundaries and designated parking
- Owner or designated steward available while guests are present
- Guest and vehicle manifest for each stay
- Published check-in, checkout, quiet-hour, fire, trash, and emergency rules
- No amplified sound, generators, or unauthorized fires
- Occupancy limits established by approved land use, fire access, sanitation,
  parking, and insurance requirements
- Stays limited to the duration allowed by the County's Recreation Camp rules

The initial demand case assumes all three sites are occupied on Friday and
Saturday nights during all eight Colorado Renaissance Festival weekends. Nightly
guest capacity, vehicle limits, other operating dates, and Site 3 capacity remain
to be determined with agency input.

## 4. Sanitation and water plan

The initial phase will not connect campground use to the residence's onsite
wastewater treatment system. The proposed system consists of:

- Commercially supplied and regularly serviced portable toilet units
- Running potable water at a dedicated handwashing sink behind the barn
- Soap, paper towels, and hygienic wastewater containment
- An approved method for collecting and disposing of sink, dishwashing, and
  other graywater; no discharge onto the ground
- Covered, animal-resistant trash containers with scheduled removal
- Service-truck access to the portable toilet, preferably within approximately
  25 feet, subject to the selected vendor's requirements

Running water does not remove the need for approved wastewater disposal. The
sink discharge must either enter an approved system or be captured and removed
by an approved method.

El Paso County Public Health should provide a written determination addressing:

- Whether commercial portable toilets are acceptable for this campground type
- Number and configuration of toilet facilities, including sex designation or
  an approved single-user arrangement
- Whether showers are required for a three-site tent campground
- Handwashing fixture and accessibility requirements
- Potable-water testing and public-water-system classification, if applicable
- Approved graywater storage, servicing, and disposal
- Required separation from wells, watercourses, food preparation, property
  lines, and campsites

## 5. Land-use and agency path

County staff has identified the use as a **Recreation Camp** in the RR-5 zoning
district. The anticipated review path is:

1. Early Assistance meeting and written agency comments
2. Special Use application
3. Site Development Plan
4. Public Health, fire district, transportation, drainage, and other referral
   reviews
5. Any building, plumbing, access, sign, or operational permits identified
   during review
6. Final inspections and approvals before accepting paid guests

The Special Use case is expected to address master-plan consistency,
neighborhood compatibility, service capacity, legal and safe access, traffic,
pollution controls, public health and safety, and compliance with other County
requirements.

## 8. Neighborhood and environmental protections

The operating plan should commit to:

- Quiet hours and prompt response to complaints
- Downward-directed, low-level, dark-sky-conscious lighting
- Defined maximum vehicles and no roadside parking
- No amplified events as part of the campground approval
- Fire restrictions that meet or exceed County and fire-district requirements
- Weather cancellation thresholds and a severe-weather refuge or closure plan
- Regular trash, sanitation, and property inspections
- Protection of existing trees and control of erosion and informal trails
- Clear enforcement and removal procedures for guests who violate rules

Neighbor concerns were significant in comparable Monument glamping cases.
Starting with a small, plainly defined tent-only operation and enforceable limits
should be central to the compatibility narrative.

### Renaissance Festival full-booking case

The 2026 Colorado Renaissance Festival ran for eight weekends. The model assumes
two paid nights per weekend, producing 16 fully occupied nights for each site.

| Pricing case | Sites 1 and 2, each/night | Site 3/night | Gross revenue for 16 nights |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | $50 | $100 | $3,200 |
| Base | $75 | $150 | $4,800 |
| High | $100 | $200 | $6,400 |

This is gross lodging revenue, before booking and payment fees, taxes, insurance,
sanitation servicing, supplies, cleaning, maintenance, marketing, refunds, or
owner labor. A preliminary annual operating allowance is **$3,000–$8,000 plus
5–15 percent of booking revenue**.

At the base startup target of about $37,900, recovering startup costs over five
festival seasons would require roughly $7,580 per year before annual operations.
After adding a $4,000 base operating allowance, the campground would need about
$11,580 gross contribution per festival season—approximately **$724 per fully
booked night across all three sites**. If Site 3 is priced at twice an individual
site, this is about **$181 per individual site and $362 for Site 3 per night**, before
percentage booking costs.

The eight-weekend campground by itself therefore should not be assumed to repay
the full permitting and development investment. Feasibility improves if:

- professional and access work remains near the low case;
- camping operates on additional approved weekends;
- approved AfterFaire or other revenue contributes to shared infrastructure;
- rates support the high case without reducing occupancy; or
- the project is justified partly as a long-term property amenity rather than a
  stand-alone short-term return.

No irreversible construction should begin until the County confirms the review
path and required plan content.

## 10. Phasing

### Phase 0 — Feasibility and Early Assistance

- Submit the concept narrative and reconciled site map
- Obtain written comments from Planning, Public Health, fire, transportation,
  drainage, and other reviewing agencies
- Confirm application class, campsite count, sanitation approach, access scope,
  and fee assumptions
- Obtain preliminary insurance and portable-sanitation vendor input

**Decision gate:** Proceed only if the permitted capacity and required capital
work support a viable small operation.

### Phase 1 — Primitive tent campground

- Prepare and submit Special Use and Site Development Plan materials
- Complete required survey, access, drainage, sanitation, fire, parking, and
  landscape work
- Adopt operating and emergency plans
- Obtain final approvals and inspections
- Open at the approved small-site capacity and monitor impacts

### Phase 2 — Operational refinement

- Review occupancy, incidents, neighbor feedback, maintenance, and economics
- Improve screening, paths, drainage, parking, or operations within the existing
  approval
- Do not expand capacity without written confirmation that the change is allowed

### Future phase — Furnished tents, yurts, or other lodging

- Begin only after Phase 1 demonstrates successful low-impact operation
- Request a pre-application determination for use classification, building and
  fire code, sanitation, accessibility, and road-impact fees
- Treat future structures as a separate approval and investment decision
